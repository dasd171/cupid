Analyze the communication patterns between the two people.

COMMUNICATION — consider:
- Reply length balance: does one person consistently write much more or less?
- Reply speed (when timestamps exist): prompt replies vs. long silences.
- Initiation ratio: who starts conversations more often?
- Question ratio: who asks follow-up questions, keeping dialogue alive?
- Dialogue continuation: do answers invite further exchange or shut it down?

EMOTIONAL ENGAGEMENT — consider:
- Emotional expression: sharing feelings, not just facts.
- Care and empathy: noticing the other person's mood or situation.
- Emotional support: comforting, encouraging, validating.
- Affectionate / flirtatious signals: warmth, teasing, compliments, pet names.
  Mark these as observed signals only — never as proof of romantic intent.

MUTUAL INTEREST — consider:
- Both-sided initiative vs. one-sided effort.
- Interest in each other's daily life, work, friends, hobbies.
- Actively creating opportunities to talk or meet.
- Remembering details the other person shared earlier.

RELATIONSHIP DIRECTION — choose exactly one:
- "romantic": clear mutual romantic signals and intent.
- "potential_romantic": genuine interest and warmth, but ambiguous or
  asymmetric; real potential with visible risks.
- "friendship": warm and engaged, but the tone and topics read platonic.
- "unclear": not enough evidence, or signals point in conflicting directions.
- "cooling_down": declining frequency, shorter replies, emotional withdrawal.

CONFLICT PATTERN — only if the conversation contains disagreements:
- Frequency of conflicts, avoidance, apologies, compromises,
  aggressive language, repair attempts after conflict.
- If no conflict is visible, score conflictResolution neutrally (~50)
  and say evidence was insufficient.

FUTURE ORIENTATION — look for:
- Future plans, trips together, meeting up, shared activities,
  long-term mentions ("next year", "someday we should…").
- Absence of any future talk is itself a (weak) signal; say so.

For every important judgment, attach evidence referencing observable
facts (e.g. "A initiates approximately 62% of conversations").
If the evidence does not support a judgment, mark confidence "low".
